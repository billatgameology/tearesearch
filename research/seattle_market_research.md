# Seattle / Eastside Chinese Tea Market Research

**Research date:** August 28, 2026  
**Business assumption:** A Chinese-founded U.S. LLC selling premium Chinese tea, guided tea experiences, take-home tea and related services.  
**Geography:** Seattle and the Eastside, with emphasis on the Chinatown-International District, Capitol Hill, Fremont/Ballard, the University District, Downtown/South Lake Union, Bellevue, Redmond and Kirkland.  
**Method:** Public-source desk research. Current prices, hours, operator status and asking-rent samples were checked on August 28, 2026 unless another date is stated.

> **Decision status:** Seattle has enough evidence to justify a paid, multi-format market test. It does not yet have enough evidence to justify a permanent lease.

## 1. Executive decision

### Recommendation

Run Seattle as a **two-lane validation market**:

1. **Eastside lane — Bellevue and Redmond:** test a polished, beginner-friendly premium experience, private groups, corporate events and gifting. Bellevue has the strongest all-around score; Redmond adds a large technology-employer base and an established specialist tea room.
2. **Seattle lane — Chinatown-International District and Ballard/Fremont:** test cultural credibility, specialist tea education, product authority and repeat retail through partner venues and pop-ups. Do not assume either neighborhood can support a new tea-only lease.

Use Downtown/South Lake Union as a corporate and visitor sales territory, not the initial public storefront. Use the University District for a fast hero drink, student discovery and festival tests. Use Capitol Hill for evening, date and alcohol-free social programming.

The March 2026 cross-lake opening of Sound Transit's 2 Line materially improves the ability to test Seattle, Bellevue and Redmond as one operating market. It does not eliminate travel time, parking, last-mile or neighborhood-specific demand risk. ([Sound Transit](https://www.soundtransit.org/blog/platform/welcome-2-eastside), [2 Line schedule](https://www.soundtransit.org/ride-with-us/routes-schedules/2-line))

### Best current concept hypothesis

A **reservation-led Chinese tea studio plus mobile event and retail business** fits the evidence better than a large walk-in lounge. A fast, tea-forward hero drink should recruit people into guided experiences and take-home tea; it should not force the whole business into high-volume boba economics.

The starting offer ladder should be tested around Seattle's observable bands:

| Offer | Observable Seattle reference | Proposed role |
|---|---:|---|
| Fast tea or milk-tea drink | About **$5-$8.50** | Low-friction trial and event throughput |
| Guided introductory tasting | About **$30-$38 per person** | Core education and acquisition offer |
| Premium occasion experience | **$55-$75 proposed test** | Dates, celebrations, private groups and gifting |
| Take-home loose-leaf tea | Commonly **$15-$100+** | Repeat purchase, gifting and margin layer |

The fast-drink band is visible at [TP Tea's Chinatown-International District menu](https://www.toasttab.com/local/order/tp-tea-king-st/item-b9-brown-sugar-pearl-w-fresh-milk-_6d8903f1-4bdb-4c5f-8154-a60966fde24c) and [Drip Tea](https://order.toasttab.com/online/drip-tea-seattle-rebook-1416-10th-avenue). The tasting band is visible at Floating Leaves, TeaFairy and Tea House Gallery. The premium band is a proposed experiment, not an established Chinese-tea market price; the local occasion ceiling is supported only indirectly by Queen Mary Tea Room's current **$64 weekday / $75 weekend** afternoon-tea pricing. ([OpenTable](https://www.opentable.com/queen-mary-tea-room), [operator menus](https://queenmarytea.com/pages/menus))

### What the online evidence supports

- Seattle has a credible specialist loose-leaf ecosystem, but only a small set of recurring, bookable Chinese or Taiwanese tea experiences.
- The current operators layer tea with retail, e-commerce, education, memberships or gatherings, private events, food, fulfillment or pop-ups. Public evidence does not support a tea-service-only revenue model.
- Bellevue and Redmond combine high household income, substantial Chinese and Chinese-language resident proxies, technology employers and direct rail access.
- The Chinatown-International District provides cultural fit and specialist incumbents but has a lower resident-income proxy, high food-service competition and regulatory review for physical changes.
- Downtown/South Lake Union has the strongest workplace and visitor structure in the model but remains unproven for leisure conversion at any particular storefront.
- Washington's recorded tea imports grew strongly from comparable January-June 2024 to 2026, while China-origin value did not keep pace. The data describe supply-chain flow, not Seattle consumer demand.

### What remains unproven

- Whether first-time customers will pay for unsweetened Chinese tea without food, milk tea or a familiar occasion frame.
- Which combination of neighborhood, message, time of day and price generates the best contribution profit.
- Whether Eastside corporate demand converts from outreach into paid deposits and repeat bookings.
- Whether experience guests repurchase loose-leaf tea within 30, 60 and 90 days.
- Whether the service can be delivered by a trained host without founder dependence.
- Whether a particular room can cover full occupancy and staffing costs under a downside volume case.

## 2. Evidence rules used in this report

The report keeps four categories separate:

- **Measured fact:** a government, operator or institutional number.
- **Observed market evidence:** a current price, format, opening, closure or operating practice visible on a primary site or corroborated by reputable reporting.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from multiple observations; it is labeled.
- **Proposed test:** a target or operating experiment for this company; it is not an industry benchmark.

Private competitors generally do not publish store-level revenue, profit, traffic, rent, capacity or retention. The “revenue layers” below are visible products and services, not estimates of revenue mix. Continued operation is evidence of durability, not proof of profitability.

ZIP Code Tabulation Areas are used as neighborhood proxies because current public jobs and establishment files can be joined consistently at that level. They do not follow retail-district boundaries. Combined-ZCTA household income is a population-weighted descriptive proxy, not a Census-published combined median.

## 3. Why Seattle is worth testing

### 3.1 Direct specialist competition is real but fragmented

The most decision-relevant operators span four different models:

| Model | Current examples | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informal specialist retail | Seattle Best Tea; Vital T Leaf | Chinese/Taiwanese tea can support tasting-led retail and e-commerce | Profitability or demand for a formal paid ceremony |
| Reservation-led instruction | Floating Leaves; TeaFairy; Tea House Gallery; Young Tea | Customers can pay about $30-$38 for guided tea | A full-time tasting-room lease |
| Community specialty cafe | Miro Tea; Friday Afternoon Tea | Tea can anchor a repeat social place when paired with broad assortment, food or community programming | Demand specifically for Chinese tea |
| Online/product authority | Crimson Lotus Tea and specialist retailers | Sourcing expertise, content and e-commerce can reach beyond local foot traffic | Reliable walk-in demand |

**Inference:** Seattle is not an empty market. The whitespace is a clearer bridge between approachable hospitality and Chinese-tea depth, with a deliberate path from one drink to an experience, then to replenishment or gifting.

### 3.2 The Eastside combines cultural, income and employer signals

The 2024 ACS five-year ZCTA proxies used in this report show median household income of about **$182,545** in Bellevue 98004 and **$163,460** in Redmond 98052. The constructed Chinese population proxy is **19.6%** and **12.1%**, respectively; Chinese spoken at home is **16.7%** and **11.0%**. These measures are demographic descriptors, not purchase intent. Exact variables and reproducible calls are in Appendix A.

Bellevue describes itself as Washington's second-largest job hub, with **65 headquarters** and **90 international firms**. Its 2026 economic plan lists roughly **14,300 Amazon**, **7,800 T-Mobile**, **5,400 Meta** and **3,800 Overlake Medical Center** workers among major employers. ([City of Bellevue](https://bellevuewa.gov/expand-bellevue), [2026 Economic Development Plan](https://bellevuewa.gov/sites/default/files/media/pdf_document/2026/cdd-26-67756-bellevue-economic-development-plan-2026-web.pdf))

Redmond lists Microsoft at **more than 52,000** local workers, Amazon at **3,000-3,500**, Meta at **2,000-2,500** and Nintendo at **1,000-1,500**. The city describes Overlake as a technology cluster with about **48,000 jobs**. Employer counts are sales-prospect signals, not confirmed tea demand. ([City of Redmond employer list](https://www.redmond.gov/943/Largest-Employers), [Overlake](https://www.redmond.gov/576/Overlake))

### 3.3 Visitor and institutional channels are large enough to test

Visit Seattle reported **39.7 million** Seattle/King County visitors in 2025, **$8.8 billion** in visitor spending, **68,623 jobs** supported and **$840 million** in state and local tax revenue. Visitors declined 0.6% and spending declined 0.3% from 2024, so this is not a simple growth story. ([Visit Seattle 2025 results](https://visitseattle.org/press/press-releases/visit-seattle-2025-annual-meeting-data/))

The Port of Seattle recorded **1.9 million cruise revenue passengers** and **298 ship calls** in 2025, with an estimated **$1.2 billion** regional impact. Seattle Center reported **11.3 million** 2025 visits. These are broad channel indicators; they do not identify people seeking tea or foot traffic near a proposed site. ([Port of Seattle](https://www.portseattle.org/news/2025-cruise-season-ends-record-passenger-numbers), [City of Seattle / Seattle Center](https://centerspotlight.seattle.gov/2026/04/09/new-report-shows-seattle-center-generates-nearly-3-3-billion-in-economic-impact-supporting-19400-jobs/))

### 3.4 Local tea education already has cultural infrastructure

- The [Northwest Tea Festival](https://seattlecenter.com/events/event-calendar/northwest-tea-festival), produced by the Puget Sound Tea Education Association, is scheduled at Seattle Center for September 26-27, 2026 and brings tastings, classes and vendors together.
- The [Seattle Asian Art Museum's Chanoyu program](https://www.seattleartmuseum.org/whats-on/programs/chanoyu-tea-culture) runs on the second and third Saturdays; the audience observes a Japanese tea presentation but is not served tea.
- The [Seattle Chinese Garden](https://www.seattlechinesegarden.org/) identifies tea among its cultural-program areas and hosts seasonal events.
- The University District's 2026 [Seattle Boba Fest](https://udistrictseattle.com/bobafest) advertised more than 20 shops and nearly 30 participants. That proves a dense discovery channel for sweet tea drinks, not unmet demand for a slow tasting.

**Inference:** Seattle already teaches consumers that tea can be culture, craft and an event. The business should collaborate with that ecosystem and distinguish Chinese tea carefully, not claim to introduce “real tea” to an uninformed city.

### 3.5 Washington has a meaningful tea supply-chain signal

U.S. Census state-import files show the following customs value for HS 0902 tea, using comparable January-June year-to-date snapshots:

| Recorded destination | Jan-Jun 2024 | Jan-Jun 2025 | Jan-Jun 2026 | 2024-2026 change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Washington, all origins | **$7.89M** | **$9.32M** | **$11.45M** | **+45.0%** |
| Washington, origin China | **$1.07M** | **$1.53M** | **$1.14M** | **+6.1%** |

Washington represented **3.59%** of U.S. tea customs value in January-June 2026. China-origin tea represented **9.92%** of Washington's tea value and fell **26.0%** from the comparable 2025 snapshot. Green-tea categories represented **47.2%**; black/partly fermented categories represented **52.8%**.

**Critical limitation:** the Census destination may be an importer, warehouse or distribution point rather than final consumption, and the data cannot track later interstate movement. This is supply-chain evidence, not Seattle sales, market share or demand. See the [Census product](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/data/ISTHSM.html), [record layout](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/reference/products/layouts/isths6.html) and [state-data limitations](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/guide/sec2.html).

### 4.3 What the competitor set says about revenue design

| Revenue layer | Local proof point | Launch implication |
|---|---|---|
| Guided public sessions | Floating Leaves, TeaFairy, Tea House Gallery | Make prepaid education a core product rather than free labor attached to retail |

### 5.3 Neighborhood readout

#### Bellevue

**Measured facts:** ZCTA 98004 has the highest cultural proxy, income and one of the highest workplace-job counts in this comparison. Bellevue has direct 2 Line service and a dense major-employer list. A current 1,718-SF cafe/restaurant sample asks **$55/SF/year**, expenses excluded, and restricts cafe/coffee operation until February 2027.

**Inference:** Bellevue is the best first paid Eastside cell for a polished introductory experience and B2B offering, but a premium lease could erase its demand advantage.

**Proposed test:** run two public sessions at a compliant partner venue and sell ten employer/private-group proposals before considering a dedicated room.

#### Redmond

**Measured facts:** ZCTA 98052 combines about 80,700 residents, high household income, 123,261 LODES workplace jobs and direct 2 Line service. TeaFairy already validates specialist tea activity. A current Redmond Way sample asks **$44/SF/year NNN**.

**Inference:** Redmond is strong for employer and repeat-resident business but not whitespace. A new company must be easier for beginners, more convenient for private groups or meaningfully differentiated in product.

**Proposed test:** compare lunchtime employer booking, weekend public tasting and take-home reorder performance against Bellevue.

#### Chinatown-International District

**Measured facts:** ZCTA 98104 has the lowest income and education proxies in the comparison but the strongest Seattle-side Chinese and Chinese-language proxies, direct 1/2 Line access and multiple incumbent tea specialists. Alterations, signs, uses and exterior work in the International Special Review District can require review. ([City of Seattle](https://www.cityofseattle.org/neighborhoods/historic-preservation/historic-districts/international-special-review-district))

**Inference:** cultural legitimacy and destination fit are strong, but “authenticity” is not automatic permission to charge a premium. The company would enter an established specialist ecosystem.

**Proposed test:** use existing venues and events, compensate partners, and measure visitor versus local conversion before evaluating a room.

#### Fremont/Ballard

**Measured facts:** the combined proxy has high education and income, extensive work-from-home and current specialist incumbents including Floating Leaves, Miro and Friday Afternoon Tea. King County Metro Route 40 connects Ballard/Fremont with South Lake Union and downtown. ([King County Metro](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/metro/routes-and-service/schedules-and-maps/schedule?route=040))

**Inference:** this is a credible repeat-community and tea-literacy market, but competition for enthusiasts is stronger than the score alone suggests.

**Proposed test:** lead with a differentiated Chinese-region flight or sourcing story, then track whether existing tea buyers switch or add the product.

#### University District

**Measured facts:** the ZCTA has about 49,800 residents, 50,350 workplace jobs, direct 1 Line service and a dense boba environment. Its household income is depressed by the student composition and should not be read like an employed-household catchment. ([1 Line](https://www.soundtransit.org/ride-with-us/routes-schedules/1-line))

**Inference:** the U District is better for affordable discovery and throughput than a premium, slow room.

**Proposed test:** one hero drink, a 20-30 minute beginner flight and student-group/event partnerships; cap menu complexity and record throughput.

#### Capitol Hill

**Measured facts:** the combined proxy has direct rail, high transit/no-car use and a dense hospitality ecosystem, while the documented Remedy/Modern Steep closure shows tea can be exposed to fixed costs and seasonality.

**Inference:** Capitol Hill's opportunity is an evening social job—date, gathering, creative program or zero-proof alternative—rather than conventional daytime tea retail.

**Proposed test:** two evening programs at a partner room with prepaid seats; compare group size, beverage attachment and weekday versus weekend contribution.

#### Downtown/South Lake Union

**Measured facts:** the combined ZCTA proxy contains about 200,364 LODES workplace jobs, the highest food-service density in the comparison and direct rail access. The public rent sample used here is a lower-confidence, older-marketed sublease.

**Inference:** the area is a sales territory for employer groups, hotels and conferences. It is not yet a defensible walk-in storefront recommendation.

**Proposed test:** sell the company to buildings and groups, not a lease: lobby pop-ups, team events, hotel packages and convention gifting.

#### Kirkland

**Measured facts:** ZCTA 98033 has roughly $182,500 median household income but lower Chinese/community and workplace concentration than Bellevue or Redmond, and no Link station.

**Inference:** Kirkland is a reasonable affluent-resident follow-on, but it lacks the first-test advantages of direct rail and the larger employer clusters.

**Proposed test:** include one hosted private group only after the Bellevue/Redmond offer is operating consistently.

## 6. Market whitespace and offer design

### 6.1 The defendable gap

The most defensible whitespace is not “Chinese tea exists nowhere in Seattle.” It is:

> **An approachable, reservation-led Chinese tea experience that preserves sourcing and brewing depth, converts a first visit into take-home use, and can travel to employers, cultural venues and private groups.**

This position sits between free specialist-retail education and a food-led afternoon tea or boba visit.

| Existing strength in market | Remaining customer friction | Proposed design response |
|---|---|---|
| Deep specialist knowledge | Beginner may not know what to choose or how to participate | Named 45-60 minute starting journey; no assumed vocabulary |
| Free informal tasting | Customer may not understand why a paid session is different | Publish duration, teas, host, takeaway and purchase credit |
| Premium leaf and teaware | High first basket and choice overload | Three-step retail ladder and a sub-$30 starter set |
| Fast milk tea / boba | Little time for origin, brewing or replenishment education | One fast, unsweetened or lightly sweet hero drink linked to the same leaf |
| Formal afternoon tea | Food and occasion may overshadow tea | Tea-first occasion format with optional light compliant pairing |
| Expert collector sessions | Narrow audience and host dependence | Beginner script first; collector sessions only after demand appears |

### 6.2 Recommended launch menu

The following is a **proposed test**, not a conclusion from market size:

| Offer | Test price | Operational rule | Primary measurement |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Hero tea drink | **$6-$8** | One tea base, hot/cold variations; avoid a large topping menu | Transactions/hour, wait time, drink-to-email and drink-to-retail conversion |
| Beginner tea journey | **$32-$38** | 45-60 minutes, three teas, prepaid, 6-10 seats | Paid fill, contribution/host-hour, rating, retail attachment |
| Private group | **$300-$450 minimum** | 60-75 minutes, deposit, defined travel and capacity | Lead-to-deposit, contribution/event, rebooking |
| Premium occasion | **$55-$75/person** | Limited dates; stronger setting, story and take-home component | Conversion versus beginner offer, gifting and referral |
| Starter retail | **$18-$30** | One tea or a small flight with simple brew guide | Attachment and 30-day reorder |
| Core retail | **$35-$75** | Traceable product story and brew support | Gross margin dollars, 30/60/90-day reorder |

The private-group and retail prices are operating hypotheses. Actual pack size, landed cost, venue fee, labor and payment expense must determine the final price.

### 6.3 Do not launch these features by default

- A full kitchen, large food menu or all-day staffing.
- A large tea catalog that cannot be sampled, explained and replenished reliably.
- A collector-focused room before the beginner funnel works.
- A health-benefit proposition. Product and marketing claims should stay within substantiated food-labeling and advertising rules.
- A long lease justified by broad Seattle tourism, employer counts or demographic proxies.

### 8.3 Sales tax and business taxes

- Washington DOR says restaurants, cafes, coffee shops and snack bars generally collect retail sales tax on food and beverages and report retailing business-and-occupation tax. The prepared-food rules include a **75% prepared-food test** that can affect how sales are treated. ([Restaurant/cafe guide](https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/restaurants-and-retailers-prepared-food/restaurants-cafes-coffee-shops-snack-bars-etc), [food-service guide](https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/food-services-and-drinking-places))
- Seattle's 2026 B&O rates are **0.00342** for retailing and **0.00658** for service and other activities. Seattle says the annual taxable-revenue threshold increased to **$2 million** effective January 1, 2026, but businesses may still have filing obligations. Classify tasting/service, retail, event and wholesale receipts correctly rather than applying one rate to all revenue. ([Seattle rates](https://seattle.gov/city-finance/business-taxes-and-licenses/business-taxes/tax-rates-and-classifications), [Seattle business taxes](https://www.seattle.gov/city-finance/business-taxes-and-licenses/business-taxes))
- Seattle's sweetened-beverage distribution tax is **$0.0175 per fluid ounce** for covered sweetened beverages. Unsweetened tea is not a sweetened beverage, but caloric sweeteners, syrups, concentrates and self-distribution can create obligations. ([Seattle tax overview](https://www.seattle.gov/city-finance/business-taxes-and-licenses/seattle-taxes/sweetened-beverage-tax), [Director's Rule 5-953](https://www.seattle.gov/city-finance/about-city-finance/directors-rules/directors-rule-5-953))

Register point-of-sale items by tax treatment and jurisdiction. A pop-up strategy still creates sales-tax, city-tax and recordkeeping duties.

### 8.5 Importing and product compliance

The fastest low-risk pilot uses tea already imported and released by a compliant U.S. supplier. Direct import should begin only after demand, supplier records, cash requirements and broker workflow are clear.

Before direct import:

1. Confirm whether each foreign food facility is registered and establish a compliant U.S. agent arrangement where required.
2. Establish Foreign Supplier Verification Program responsibility and written supplier-verification records.
3. File FDA prior notice for each shipment within the applicable timing rules.
4. Use a customs broker to determine the 10-digit HTS classification, ordinary duty and any current Section 301 treatment.
5. Screen the supplier, product and origin against FDA import alerts and detention history.
6. Verify label identity, net contents, responsible firm, origin marking and any required nutrition/allergen treatment for the finished pack.
7. Retain lot, invoice, test, shipment and customer-traceability records; define recall and complaint procedures.

Primary starting points: [FDA importing food](https://www.fda.gov/importing-food-products-united-states), [FDA prior notice](https://www.fda.gov/industry/fda-import-process/division-targeting-and-analysis-prior-notice), [FDA FSVP guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/118241/download?attachment=), [FDA import alerts](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/default.html), [USTR Section 301 search](https://ustr.gov/issue-areas/enforcement/section-301-investigations/search) and [USITC 2026 HTS Revision 17](https://www.usitc.gov/2026_hts_revision_17).

Tariff and admissibility treatment can change and may be supplier-specific. This report intentionally does not hard-code a landed-duty percentage.

## 9. Twelve-week paid-validation plan

### 9.1 Test matrix

Use the same core tea, host script, landing-page structure and cost definitions so neighborhood differences are interpretable.

| Cell | Neighborhood / channel | Offer | Key question |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Bellevue public partner venue | Beginner journey at $32-$38 | Will affluent Eastside beginners prepay? |
| E2 | Redmond public partner venue | Same beginner journey | Does resident/tech proximity improve paid fill or retail attachment? |
| S1 | Chinatown-International District partner | Specialist/cultural flight | Can the brand add value beside strong incumbents? |
| S2 | Ballard/Fremont partner | Same tea, more sourcing-led message | Do existing tea buyers attach and reorder more? |
| F1 | U District or suitable event | $6-$8 hero drink plus short upsell | Can a fast product recruit future tasting customers? |
| N1 | Capitol Hill evening | $38-$55 social tea program | Is the evening occasion stronger than daytime education? |
| B1 | Bellevue/Redmond/SLU employers | $300-$450 private minimum | Can outreach convert to paid deposits and rebookings? |
| P1 | Best-performing cell | $55-$75 premium occasion | Is there an occasion ceiling above the beginner offer? |

### 9.2 Weekly plan

#### Weeks 1-2: legal, food-safety and measurement foundation

- Confirm entity, UBI, city endorsements, seller tax and permit path.
- Use domestically available compliant tea for the pilot.
- Freeze two beginner scripts and one hero drink; cost every serving and minute.
- Build source-tagged landing pages, waiver/allergy language where appropriate, CRM and refund/no-show policy.
- Recruit at least four compliant venue partners across both lanes.

**Deliverable:** written permit determination for the actual menus, fully costed offers and live prepaid booking.

#### Weeks 3-4: first matched public tests

- Run one Bellevue, one Redmond, one Chinatown-International District and one Ballard/Fremont session.
- Hold duration, tea set, capacity and base price constant.
- Record invitation source, payment, attendance, timing, direct cost, rating, attachment and reorder consent.

**Decision use:** separate neighborhood effect from novelty and founder-network sales.

#### Weeks 5-6: recruitment and throughput

- Run the hero drink in the U District, Chinatown-International District or a permitted event.
- Offer a traceable tasting credit rather than a general discount.
- Measure drinks/hour, maximum queue, service errors, gross contribution, email capture and later booking.

**Decision use:** determine whether a fast front door exists without building a boba menu.

#### Weeks 7-8: private-group and employer selling

- Contact at least 30 qualified employer, building, hotel or association prospects.
- Send a concrete menu, minimum, travel radius, capacity, setup needs and deposit link.
- Run paid private events when booked; no free “exposure” events unless they are explicitly budgeted acquisition experiments.

**Decision use:** estimate qualified-lead-to-deposit rate, sales cycle and contribution per event.

#### Weeks 9-10: repeatability

- Repeat the top two public cells with the same acquisition channel.
- Offer 30-day replenishment and a second-session reason.
- Train a second host and compare timing, ratings, retail attachment and customer questions.

**Decision use:** distinguish repeatable demand from a founder-led launch spike.

#### Week 11: price and occasion

- Run one premium $55-$75 occasion and one specialist/collector session.
- Keep the customer promise visibly different from the beginner journey.

**Decision use:** test price ladder and segment depth without redesigning the whole business.

#### Week 12: cohort review

- Close 30-day outcomes for early cohorts.
- Rank cells by contribution per host-hour, trackable acquisition cost, repeat/reorder and partner repeatability.
- Choose stop, iterate, continue mobile, pursue a small appointment studio or begin site diligence.

### 9.3 Proposed gates

These are **company decision rules**, not industry benchmarks.

| Gate | Proposed threshold by end of pilot | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---|
| Breadth | At least **8 paid sessions**, **4 venues** and **2 distinct neighborhoods** | Reduces one-venue and one-audience bias |
| Paid demand | At least **120 paid individual purchases**, or **80 individuals plus 4 paid private groups** | Requires more than interviews and likes |
| Public fill | At least **65% paid seat fill** across repeated public sessions | Tests schedule, message and price together |
| Network independence | No more than **25%** of customers from the founder's personal network | Reduces false launch confidence |
| Guided contribution | At least **55% contribution margin** after tea, consumables, venue, payments, event labor and travel | Protects against attractive but labor-heavy sales |
| Retail contribution | At least **65% contribution margin** on take-home goods before general overhead | Tests product economics separately |
| Labor productivity | At least **$90 contribution per host-hour** for public sessions and **$150** for private groups | Captures setup, delivery and cleanup pressure |
| Acquisition | Trackable CAC recoverable on the first transaction or within **60 days** | Avoids growth that depends on unrecovered promotion |
| Repeat | At least **20% 30-day repeat or reorder** among eligible early cohorts | Tests durability beyond the event |
| Attachment | At least **25% retail attachment** and **40% qualified email opt-in** | Tests the experience-to-product funnel |
| Channel repeatability | At least **3 willing repeat venue/B2B partners** and **2 paid rebookings or deposits** | Tests access without a lease |
| Service transfer | Second-host customer rating at least **4.6/5** with no more than a **10% relative decline** from founder-led sessions | Tests whether the experience is a system |

If the pilot does not produce enough eligible 30-day customers by Week 12, extend only the measurement window, not the spending scope.

### 9.4 Lease gate

Do not sign a conventional storefront lease until all of the following are true:

- the same catchment has produced at least three consecutive profitable monthly cohorts;
- non-founder hosts can deliver the experience;
- the site has a written use/permit path and build-out budget;
- a downside case covers base rent, NNN/CAM, utilities, insurance, minimum staffing and debt or build-out obligations with at least **1.5x fixed-cost coverage**;
- walk-in assumptions are supported by manual counts and comparable paid conversion at the exact block;
- the company has enough cash for deposits, improvements, inventory, launch losses and contingency without relying on optimistic sales.

The 1.5x buffer is a proposed company risk rule, not a published industry standard.

## 11. What online research can and cannot establish

### What this report establishes

- A dated census of visible specialist and adjacent operators, their public formats, prices and status.
- Evidence that Seattle customers can buy guided tea around $30-$38 and occasion tea at substantially higher prices.
- Transparent neighborhood proxies for residents, cultural fit, workplace jobs, food-service density, access, wages and current asking rents.
- Specific employer, visitor, institutional and event channels that can be approached.
- A current operational checklist and a measurable pilot design.

### What must be learned offline

- True foot traffic and direction by daypart on a specific block.
- Competitor seat fill, repeat rate, revenue, margin and landlord terms.
- Whether partner organizations want the offer and on what commercial terms.
- Tea preferences and willingness to pay among people who actually transact.
- Site-specific permitted use, plumbing, power, HVAC, accessibility and construction cost.
- Whether the proposed host, script and tea deliver a differentiated experience.

### Fieldwork still required before any lease

1. Complete three dayparts of 15-minute manual counts at each finalist site on two weekdays and one weekend.
2. Mystery-shop every high-signal specialist format with a consistent rubric.
3. Obtain five small-space broker quotes with all pass-throughs and permitted use.
4. Submit the exact menu/process to King County and the relevant city for written direction.
5. Interview at least ten venue/B2B buyers and ask for a paid deposit, not only interest.
6. Run the 12-week paid plan and preserve transaction-level cohort data.

## 12. Bottom line

Seattle is a **go for disciplined paid validation** and a **no-go for a lease today**.

The best evidence-backed path is:

1. launch matched beginner sessions in Bellevue, Redmond, the Chinatown-International District and Ballard/Fremont;
2. sell private groups into Bellevue, Redmond and South Lake Union;
3. test one fast hero drink in a high-discovery setting;
4. prove retail attachment and 30-day replenishment;
5. train a second host;
6. consider a small appointment studio only after the contribution, repeat and fixed-cost gates are met.

Bellevue is the best first mainstream/Eastside test, Redmond the best complementary employer-and-resident test, the Chinatown-International District the strongest Seattle cultural/specialist test, and Downtown/South Lake Union the best B2B territory. None is yet a justified permanent site.

## Appendix A. Neighborhood scorecard methodology and evidence

### A1. Exact scoring method

Each subindex is 0-100. Every component is clipped to that range before weighting.

- **Culture:** 60% Chinese proxy, where 20% = 100; 40% Chinese spoken at home, where 17% = 100.
- **Purchasing:** 55% median household income, linearly scaled from $60,000 = 0 to $180,000 = 100; 45% bachelor's degree or higher, scaled from 45% = 0 to 80% = 100.
- **Daytime:** 65% log-scaled workplace jobs, from 10,000 = 0 to 200,000 = 100; 35% jobs/resident, where 3.0 = 100.
- **Ecosystem/headroom:** 65% NAICS 722 establishments per 10,000 residents, scaled from 30 = 0 to 100 = 100; 35% inverse NAICS 722515 saturation, where 4 = 100 and 18 = 0.
- **Access:** analyst index based on documented rail/bus access, walkability implications of ACS transit/no-car measures and parking/last-mile friction.
- **Cost:** 75% inverse representative asking rent, where $25/SF/year = 100 and $55 = 0; 25% inverse general wage floor, where $17.13 = 100 and $21.30 = 0.
- **Testability:** analyst index based on identified venues, events, employer channels and ability to run a reversible test.

Scenario weights:

| Subindex | Community storefront | Mainstream flagship | Pop-up / B2B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Culture | 30% | 10% | 10% |
| Purchasing | 15% | 25% | 20% |
| Daytime | 10% | 20% | 25% |
| Access | 10% | 15% | 15% |
| Cost | 20% | 10% | 10% |
| Ecosystem/headroom | 10% | 10% | 5% |
| Testability | 5% | 10% | 15% |

Analyst access inputs were: Chinatown-International District 95; Capitol Hill 92; Fremont/Ballard 72; University District 95; Downtown/South Lake Union 98; Bellevue 90; Redmond 85; Kirkland 62.

Analyst testability inputs were: Chinatown-International District 93; Capitol Hill 80; Fremont/Ballard 85; University District 92; Downtown/South Lake Union 88; Bellevue 92; Redmond 90; Kirkland 70.

## Appendix C. Source, status and model cautions

- Business status, hours, prices and booking availability can change; recheck every operator before field visits or publication.
- Young Tea's indexed $35 tasting page and its current Bellevue-only tasting notice do not establish a current Bellevue price.
- New Century Tea Gallery lacks an operator-controlled current source and is flagged lower confidence.
- Floating Leaves' roasting pause is not a shop closure.
- Vital T Leaf's official language establishes a two-location footprint, but exact current addresses should be confirmed before a census visit.
- A listing being online does not prove the space is still available. Asking rates are neither executed rent nor total occupancy cost.
- Bellevue's sampled listing contains an operating restriction; it cannot be treated as immediately available cafe space.
- The South Lake Union sample has an old marketing date and short sublease horizon, so it is low confidence.
- CBP and LODES are the most recent compatible public small-area datasets used here, but both are 2023 vintage.
- ACS estimates have sampling error. ZCTAs are neighborhood proxies and combined-ZCTA median income is not an official combined median.
- Employer headcounts, visitor volumes, cruise passengers and Seattle Center attendance are channel indicators, not addressable-market estimates.
- Public event capacity or a sold-out event is not a forecast of recurring demand.
- No competitor revenue, profitability, rent, repeat rate or customer-acquisition cost is inferred from continued operation.
- Import customs value is not retail sales or local consumption.
- Scoring weights and normalization endpoints are explicit judgments. They support comparison and test design, not site underwriting.
- Proposed pilot gates are management rules. They are not presented as published sector benchmarks.
- All partnership targets are unconfirmed prospects, not endorsements.
