# Prior attempts to build a Chinese-tea business in the United States

I reviewed traditional Chinese teahouses, specialist tea importers, appointment-only tasting rooms, modern hybrids, and larger specialty-tea chains.

The main conclusion is:

> **Americans have repeatedly shown that they will buy Chinese tea, education, tastings, teaware, and community experiences. What has proved much harder is supporting a large, full-time retail space through ordinary tea service alone.**

A closed teahouse does not necessarily mean the underlying tea business failed. In many cases, the room closed while the brand continued through online sales, wholesale, subscriptions, appointments, classes, or events.

There is also an important evidence limitation: nearly all traditional teahouses are private businesses. We can observe their openings, closures, relocations, products, prices, and public explanations, but we generally cannot see their profit-and-loss statements. I therefore distinguish between **confirmed causes** and **my analysis of the likely business mechanism**.

---

# 1. Seven Cups: the strongest long-term precedent

## What they attempted

Seven Cups started by selling Chinese tea at a Tucson farmers’ market. The founders built a local customer base before opening their teahouse in 2004. They source directly from more than 30 Chinese producers and sell through the Tucson teahouse, e-commerce, wholesale, and tea-brokerage services. The founders reported in 2022 that they had sold to customers in more than 100 countries, and the company’s current store lists newly harvested 2026 teas. ([Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/about-us/?srsltid=AfmBOopy2thzyEdfbBo-Q597KKz6ibN7PfhDrL5EHPsK-O_-gB-pmUFj); [Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/2022/01/happy-chinese-new-year-from-seven-cups/?srsltid=AfmBOoo5Zl7F7FkyyoQCCXwIT8uE55Pit-xac9QSqYXNyzburVFTi9qW))

## Current progress

It is still operating more than two decades later. More importantly, the physical teahouse is only one part of the operation. The company also supplies cafés, restaurants, coffee shops, and other tea businesses. ([Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/about-us/?srsltid=AfmBOopy2thzyEdfbBo-Q597KKz6ibN7PfhDrL5EHPsK-O_-gB-pmUFj); [Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/2022/01/happy-chinese-new-year-from-seven-cups/?srsltid=AfmBOoo5Zl7F7FkyyoQCCXwIT8uE55Pit-xac9QSqYXNyzburVFTi9qW))

## Why it appears to have survived

Seven Cups followed the correct sequence:

1. Establish expertise and supplier relationships.
2. Sell at a low-cost venue.
3. Build a real local community.
4. Open a teahouse after demand already exists.
5. Add national e-commerce, wholesale, and brokerage.

The teahouse is not expected to carry the entire company by itself. It acts as a cultural center, showroom, educational space, and local community hub while other channels expand the reachable market.

### Lesson

**This is the closest positive precedent for the proposed business.**

It also challenges the blanket assumption that the first store must be in a top coastal city. Tucson is not an obvious premium-tea market. The store worked because the founders built local demand before taking on the full storefront—not because demographic data declared Tucson a perfect city.

# 2. West China Tea: powerful community demand, difficult economics

## What they attempted

West China Tea grew out of founder So-Han Fan’s Austin tea community. He initially served tea free twice a week, sold leaves from a friend’s jewelry studio, and created tea lounges at festivals, parties, and meetups. The first permanent location, opened in 2014, was only about 200 square feet. Within two years, the business expanded into a 3,000-square-foot teahouse featuring classes, events, alcohol-free late-night parties, retail products, and community programming. ([Voyage Austin](https://voyageaustin.com/interview/hidden-gems-meet-so-han-fan-of-west-china-tea/))

## What happened

The larger space became a significant financial obligation. The founder described persistent constraints involving rent, inventory, hiring, and infrastructure. When the pandemic eliminated in-person service, the company was still responsible for an expensive space and survived by pivoting rapidly to e-commerce. ([Voyage Austin](https://voyageaustin.com/interview/hidden-gems-meet-so-han-fan-of-west-china-tea/))

The business was later forced to leave its longtime location because of the I-35 highway expansion. After an eleven-month interruption, it reopened on East 7th Street in May 2025. Because all of its tea and most of its teaware come from China, tariff changes also created a major cost threat. ([KEYE](https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/90-day-tariff-pause-brings-temporary-relief-to-austin-tea-house-preparing-to-re-open))

## Current progress

West China Tea remains active in 2026. Its current model includes:

- A physical Austin teahouse
- E-commerce
- A $30 monthly tea subscription
- A $130 monthly teahouse membership
- A $1,300 annual membership
- Paid flights, classes, private service, events, and space rentals
- Long opening hours and community events

The membership includes repeated pots of tea, product discounts, and access to special inventory. ([West China Tea](https://westchinatea.com/?srsltid=AfmBOoqU2wZf2MyYwV0pKYVd-1-ry1YtTB8uFbuzAeMteD7OoJUBMrad); [West China Tea](https://westchinatea.com/yearly-teahouse-membership/?srsltid=AfmBOooEAl-YR6DIa_uq3j07OZwUaynXQ8AazgF4p1tMOtx4hywKnoNW); [West China Tea](https://westchinatea.com/subscriptions/?srsltid=AfmBOooYah2bqwvryMxF6su8h7Z0QnMcbIi1AJIG2qF8sJI6OvdnJZHu); [West China Tea](https://westchinatea.com/reservations/?srsltid=AfmBOoqOzSwc03eTGDSnaxqlAfn1aekcjpyp14bdxeaQk-bMkxw4CxZQ))

## Failure analysis

West China Tea has **not failed**, but it shows how financially fragile a successful community-oriented teahouse can be.

Demand and community were real. Yet survival required:

- A founder with a strong public identity
- Years of community formation
- E-commerce
- Membership revenue
- Events
- A forced relocation
- Outside financing and support
- Continuous adaptation
- The ability to survive a long period without physical-store income

The 3,000-square-foot expansion created a much harder business than the original 200-square-foot room. The community justified the mission, but community popularity did not automatically remove rent and capital risk.

### Lesson

**A large community space may be the eventual vision, but it is a dangerous first format.**

# 3. Tea Habitat: the specialist product did not match ordinary walk-in traffic

## What they attempted

Tea Habitat built a highly specialized business around rare Chinese teas, particularly single-tree dancong oolong. In a 2009 profile, the majority of its dancong business was already conducted online. Most casual walk-in shoppers wanted ordinary iced tea rather than the rare teas the owner had selected. The owner herself was a central part of the experience: host, teacher, advisor, expert brewer, and storyteller. ([Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/blogs/art-of-tea/at-tea-habitat-tea-connoisseurship-is-taken-to-the-extreme?srsltid=AfmBOopoU-JHp60okzo3wP-eVk67czDsXKfRR0kSyI6hAKhn413zgRXy))

## Current progress

Tea Habitat did not disappear. It evolved into an online, wholesale, and appointment-based model in Alhambra. Its current tasting room requires reservations and sells guided five-tea tastings for $50 per person. ([Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/collections/tea-tasting?srsltid=AfmBOorWC8qY_FGPo1iRiOf6NUK9nXO67B9U8ieUBfmYTtObjIpBzcRH); [Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/pages/contact?srsltid=AfmBOopjjDYIi-_Sc4R5UvclPMo2dUh81Z5snvnwRZqd4Pl3t-lcDxhu); [Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/products/regular-tea-tasting?srsltid=AfmBOoqVqO-TQ4GNCwXcYePn3P31Khr7R5dYYHVCl7xBgqZTfheERXWl))

## Failure analysis

This is one of the clearest examples of **customer-channel mismatch**:

- The product attracted serious enthusiasts nationally.
- The physical location attracted many people who simply wanted a familiar cold drink.
- The founder’s expertise created enormous value, but only for customers who actively wanted that expertise.
- Random foot traffic was not necessarily qualified demand.

Tea Habitat solved this by screening customers through an appointment and charging explicitly for the expert-led experience.

### Lesson

Do not pay premium rent just to attract people who want a $6 iced drink when the actual product is a $50 guided tasting and a $100-plus take-home tea purchase.

A compact appointment studio may be more economically aligned with serious Chinese tea than a conventional open-door café.

# 4. Té Company: wholesale and online before a tiny tearoom

## What they attempted

Té Company was founded in 2012 around Taiwanese tea sourced directly from small farms. It initially built an online and wholesale business, supplying restaurants before opening its first West Village tearoom in 2015. Tea is complemented by Taiwanese-inspired snacks, particularly its well-known pineapple cakes. ([Our Story](https://tecompanytea.com/pages/our-story?srsltid=AfmBOoqr_-9-3NxcPxSlRVNYESS1QQHbUMTikCHO-28toEqmtVzXyArF&utm_source=chatgpt.com); [New York: A True Taste of Taiwanese Tea at Té Company |](https://populayer.wordpress.com/2016/04/19/new-york-a-true-taste-of-taiwanese-tea-at-te-company/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Té Company](https://www.brooklynnavyyard.org/tenants/te-company/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Current progress

Té currently has two New York tearooms, in the West Village and East Village. The company deliberately operates small spaces with limited seating and a waitlist system. It combines table service and takeaway tea with loose-leaf retail, teaware, gift sets, subscriptions, sweet and savory snacks, corporate gifting, and events. ([Té Company](https://tecompanytea.com/pages/tea-shop?srsltid=AfmBOopxljRdLFOfjtyhVcJe6ZELttT6bQGZCTyTTPDSTSVZJyGxZkJw))

## Why this approach appears stronger

The company did not begin with an expensive hospitality operation. It first developed:

- A differentiated product
- Supplier relationships
- Restaurant customers
- Online customers
- Brand credibility

Only after that did it add a small physical room.

The snacks are also not incidental. They raise the transaction value, create reasons for repeat visits, and make unfamiliar tea more accessible without turning the business into a conventional restaurant.

### Lesson

**Wholesale and e-commerce can precede the store. A first room can be tiny, selective, and demand-constrained rather than large and dependent on constant foot traffic.**

# 5. Steep LA: traditional tea alone was expanded into a broader hospitality business

## What they attempted

Steep LA offers daytime Chinese tea ceremony sets currently priced around $35 or $45, accompanied by small dishes and desserts. Beginning in late 2020, it added Steep After Dark, featuring tea-based cocktails, nonalcoholic beverages, Asian-inspired food, and evening events. That program became permanent. The company also offers a recurring tea-ceremony box. ([Steep LA](https://steepla.com/pages/steep-after-dark); [Steep LA](https://steepla.com/); [Steep LA](https://steepla.com/products/monthly-box))

## Current progress

The business remains active in 2026 with daytime tea, evening hospitality, food, alcohol, subscriptions, product sales, and special programming. ([TAP-LA](https://tapla.wildapricot.org/event-6771234); [Time Out Worldwide](https://www.timeout.com/los-angeles/restaurants/steep-la))

## Why the hybrid matters

Steep LA is monetizing the same space during multiple dayparts:

- Daytime tea ceremony
- Retail sales
- Food and dessert
- Evening drinks
- Social gatherings
- Events
- Subscription products

That broadens the customer base and increases the number of reasons to visit.

## Tradeoff

This is operationally more complex than a pure teahouse. Food and alcohol introduce additional labor, permits, training, inventory, and management burdens. It also changes the brand from purely traditional tea education into a modern hospitality interpretation.

### Lesson

A Chinese-tea concept can become commercially more accessible by adding food, social occasions, and evening use—but the result is a more complicated restaurant-and-bar operation.

# 6. Ching Ching Cha: popular and beloved, but unable to absorb a 48% rent increase

## What they attempted

Ching Ching Cha operated in Georgetown, Washington, D.C., from 1998 through January 2023. It deliberately created a slow, quiet environment. Regular customers could stay for hours, and the owner resisted designing the experience around fast turnover. ([The Georgetown Voice](https://georgetownvoice.com/2023/02/03/for-authenticity-ching-ching-cha-stay-it-had-to-leave/))

## What happened

The Georgetown landlord raised the rent by 48%. The owner determined that preserving the original location would require changing the nature of the business and sharply increasing revenue. She instead moved to a smaller Dupont Circle space. ([The Georgetown Voice](https://georgetownvoice.com/2023/02/03/for-authenticity-ching-ching-cha-stay-it-had-to-leave/))

The current location remains open as a tea shop, pottery gallery, and intimate tea space, but it no longer serves food. ([ching ching CHA](https://www.chingchingcha.com/); [ching ching CHA](https://www.chingchingcha.com/hours-location))

## Failure analysis

This was not a failure of cultural relevance. Customers loved the space. It was a failure of **real-estate economics versus the intended experience**.

A true teahouse encourages customers to slow down. That produces:

- Long occupancy
- Low table turnover
- Relatively low beverage tickets
- A ceiling on hourly revenue per seat

The exact thing that makes the experience culturally meaningful can make it difficult to support aggressive rent.

### Lesson

**A beloved teahouse can be viable at one rent and impossible at another.**

The lease is not just an administrative detail. It determines whether the founder can preserve the desired culture or must turn the room into a high-turnover restaurant.

# 7. Tea Drunk: the storefront closed, but the knowledge business survived

## What they attempted

Tea Drunk operated an East Village teahouse for approximately eight years. Its lease expired in August 2021 after the pandemic had already pushed the company toward online education and community programming. The company explicitly described the closure as a transition rather than the end of the brand. ([EV Grieve](https://evgrieve.com/2021/08/last-weekend-for-tea-drunk-on-7th-street.html))

## Current progress

Tea Drunk now sells premium teas online and operates an educational tea club starting at $50, guided tastings, classes, and private in-person or virtual events. Virtual private events currently begin at a minimum of $500 per hour before tea and shipping costs. ([Tea Drunk](https://www.teadrunk.com/?srsltid=AfmBOor8QPhMpVtDM81BhrzMoeuTZ-o-6QA3YBeae1FCH06lrGM6R3DT); [Tea Drunk](https://www.teadrunk.com/pages/tea-tastings-by-tea-drunk?srsltid=AfmBOoqbkSwdEkUxW92MkWLNGbumuYK-IfOQOoPOuCpH6TOIzs1iYa6f); [Tea Drunk](https://www.teadrunk.com/pages/educational-tea-club?srsltid=AfmBOooiYNf906sc4ilMG5ITOICbXXjeBfdEl2i_gDJE0LMgoMtDCDdY); [Tea Drunk](https://www.teadrunk.com/pages/private-event?srsltid=AfmBOoozxHjrU9OZOVhdqUf5jf16DyPZaukrkXPH871pEqs5iBMlK48d))

## Failure analysis

The physical location ended, but the valuable assets remained:

- Founder expertise
- Audience
- Reputation
- Educational content
- Supplier access
- Premium inventory
- Community relationships

The company separated those assets from the fixed cost of an East Village storefront.

### Lesson

The business may actually be **expertise plus product**, with the teahouse only one possible delivery mechanism.

# 8. Denong Tea: storefront retreat followed by supply-chain interruption

## What they attempted

Denong created a highly premium pu-erh-focused U.S. operation, including an elaborate Pasadena retail space. After more than a decade at the original location, the company ended the physical retail operation in April 2024 while initially retaining online service. ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/C7Xt-2jPxqG/))

## Current progress

In May 2026, Denong temporarily suspended U.S. sales. Its official explanation was not a lack of customers: Hong Kong freight carriers had stopped accepting food shipments to the United States, existing U.S. inventory had largely sold out, and replenishment was no longer possible. ([Denong Tea](https://www.denongtea.com/pages/temporary-pause-in-u-s-shipments); [Denong Tea](https://www.denongtea.com/))

## Failure analysis

Two separate risks are visible:

**The confirmed problem in 2026:** an operation dependent on a narrow international replenishment route could not obtain inventory.

**The unconfirmed issue behind the 2024 storefront closure:** Denong has not publicly released enough financial information to establish why the Pasadena room ended. It would be speculation to claim that Americans rejected the tea.

However, the operating model was inherently exposed to:

- A narrow product category
- Premium pricing
- A substantial customer-education burden
- Inventory concentrated in China
- International freight and trade-policy risk

### Lesson

A culturally authentic supply chain can also become a single point of failure. The business needs multiple import routes, safety stock, and preferably some inventory already held by a qualified U.S. importer.

## The Green Teahouse

After 16 years, The Green Teahouse closed its Westfarms Mall location on August 24, 2025 and continued through its online store. The owner publicly described the move as a shift toward the growing online business; no detailed store-level financial cause was disclosed. ([The Green Teahouse](https://www.thegreenteahouse.com/); [Hartford Business](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/the-green-teahouse-at-westfarms-mall-announces-decision-to-close/); [CT Insider](https://www.ctinsider.com/westhartford/article/west-hartford-ct-green-teahouse-westfarms-mall-20825972.php))

## Samovar Tea Lounge

Samovar grew to several San Francisco locations but closed its physical lounges during the pandemic. It continued selling tea and classes online, while the founders converted some former spaces into a different restaurant concept. ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2020/08/samovar-tea-locations-go-into-covid-19-hibernation-as-company-emerges-online-with-classes/); [Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2021/10/same-owners-different-game-how-covid-19-forced-businesses-to-reinvent/); [Samovar Tea](https://www.samovartea.com/pages/store-locator?srsltid=AfmBOoqTGn9vUjcu8OP-xvmC3oql5iSVt-usGES6VFpqOlsChUKOYeBp))

## Tao of Tea

Tao of Tea has operated since 1997 but has opened and later retired several different locations and formats. Its durable core is now the Belmont teahouse, a separate retail “Leaf Room,” online sales, and wholesale operations. ([The Tao Of Tea](https://taooftea.com/teahouses/belmont-teahouse/); [The Tao Of Tea](https://taooftea.com/); [The Tao Of Tea](https://taooftea.com/teahouses/))

### Combined lesson

These companies did not necessarily lose all demand for tea. They **pruned physical channels while preserving e-commerce, wholesale, education, or a smaller core location**.

That repeated pattern is meaningful.

# 10. Large specialty-tea chains: what happened when the concept scaled aggressively

These are not authentic Chinese-tea businesses, but they provide valuable evidence about the economics of selling specialty loose-leaf tea through retail stores.

## Teavana

Starbucks announced in 2017 that all 379 Teavana stores would close. The company said the primarily mall-based stores had been persistently underperforming and that merchandising changes and redesigned stores had not reversed the trend. ([Starbucks Investor Relations](https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2017/Starbucks-Reports-Record-Q3-Financial-and-Operating-Results-Company-Announces-Strategic-Actions-to-Advance-Growth-Agenda-and-Increase-Returns/default.aspx))

This is the clearest documented case of the physical specialty-tea retail model failing at scale.

## American Tea Room

American Tea Room expanded into three Southern California locations offering retail products, tea service, lounge seating, and workspace. In November 2017 it abruptly closed all three while announcing that it would reevaluate physical retail and continue online. The company did not publicly disclose detailed store economics. ([Eater Los Angeles](https://la.eater.com/2017/11/29/16715288/american-tea-room-closed-shutter-all-locations))

My interpretation is that opening several expensive lounge-style locations before proving repeatable unit economics multiplied the fixed-cost exposure. That is an inference, not a confirmed statement from the company.

## DAVIDsTEA

DAVIDsTEA entered creditor protection in 2020, closed all 42 U.S. stores and many Canadian stores, and shifted toward e-commerce and wholesale. It retained only a much smaller Canadian retail footprint. ([DavidsTea to close 82 Canadian stores amid coronavirus ...](https://globalnews.ca/news/7160002/davidstea-canadian-store-closures-coronavirus/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [DAVIDsTEA successfully completes its restructuring under ...](https://www.fasken.com/en/experience/2020/07/davidstea-restructuring-under-the-companies-creditors-arrangement-act?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

The important follow-up is that the company did not disappear. In fiscal 2025, it returned to profitability with approximately C$61 million in sales and C$2.9 million in net income. Its revenue was spread across online, retail, and wholesale channels, and it has begun selectively adding Canadian stores again. ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/davidstea-q4-financial-statements-1-davidst-davidstea-inc-audit-2026-fs-richter-ce7f59d3d08ffe22); [DAVIDsTEA](https://davidstea.com/pages/press-releases?srsltid=AfmBOorNXuFYu_bv_NOjLgrSzzPdLIK5zKHX5lqDetiEZnocMeD4UQHi))

This suggests that **stores were not inherently useless; the oversized store network and lease obligations were the problem**.

## T2 Tea

T2 exited the United States and United Kingdom in 2023 to concentrate on markets closer to its Australian base. In March 2026, it also announced the closure of its remaining Singapore stores. It currently lists stores only in Australia and New Zealand. ([Inside Retail](https://insideretail.com.au/stores/openings-closings/t2-tea-to-exit-singapore-closing-three-remaining-stores-202603); [T2 Tea](https://www.t2tea.com/pages/stores); [The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/singapore/smes/australian-tea-brand-t2-tea-shuts-all-singapore-outlets-it-looks-reset-asia-retail-strategy))

The company has not publicly provided enough detail to isolate each location’s economics, but its retreat shows that attractive branding and a globally recognized specialty-tea concept do not automatically produce a sustainable international physical-store network.

# What actually caused failures or retreats

## 1. Slow experience versus expensive rent

This is the most fundamental contradiction.

A meaningful tea session may last one or two hours. A coffee shop can sell several drinks through the same seat during that period, and a quick-service beverage store may not need seating at all.

Ching Ching Cha provides direct evidence: customers stayed for hours, the owner valued that behavior, and the concept could not absorb a 48% rent increase. ([The Georgetown Voice](https://georgetownvoice.com/2023/02/03/for-authenticity-ching-ching-cha-stay-it-had-to-leave/))

**Diagnosis:** The store is selling time and space but may not be charging enough for either.

A guided session should therefore be priced as an experience, not merely as a pot of leaves and hot water.

---

## 2. Opening a store before building the market

The strongest survivors built demand before or outside the store:

- Seven Cups used a farmers’ market.
- West China Tea used free services, festivals, meetups, and a 200-square-foot first room.
- Té Company began online and in wholesale.
- Tea Habitat found most of its serious customers online. ([Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/about-us/?srsltid=AfmBOopy2thzyEdfbBo-Q597KKz6ibN7PfhDrL5EHPsK-O_-gB-pmUFj); [Voyage Austin](https://voyageaustin.com/interview/hidden-gems-meet-so-han-fan-of-west-china-tea/); [New York: A True Taste of Taiwanese Tea at Té Company |](https://populayer.wordpress.com/2016/04/19/new-york-a-true-taste-of-taiwanese-tea-at-te-company/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/blogs/art-of-tea/at-tea-habitat-tea-connoisseurship-is-taken-to-the-extreme?srsltid=AfmBOopoU-JHp60okzo3wP-eVk67czDsXKfRR0kSyI6hAKhn413zgRXy))

American Tea Room moved in the opposite direction by quickly operating three lounge-style locations. Teavana and DAVIDsTEA accumulated large leased networks. ([Eater Los Angeles](https://la.eater.com/2017/11/29/16715288/american-tea-room-closed-shutter-all-locations); [Starbucks Investor Relations](https://investor.starbucks.com/news/financial-releases/news-details/2017/Starbucks-Reports-Record-Q3-Financial-and-Operating-Results-Company-Announces-Strategic-Actions-to-Advance-Growth-Agenda-and-Increase-Returns/default.aspx); [DavidsTea to close 82 Canadian stores amid coronavirus ...](https://globalnews.ca/news/7160002/davidstea-canadian-store-closures-coronavirus/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Diagnosis:** The lease was treated as the beginning of demand creation rather than the result of proven demand.

---

## 3. Ordinary foot traffic is not necessarily the right customer

Tea Habitat’s experience is unusually revealing: the serious national customers bought rare tea online, while many walk-ins simply wanted iced tea. ([Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/blogs/art-of-tea/at-tea-habitat-tea-connoisseurship-is-taken-to-the-extreme?srsltid=AfmBOopoU-JHp60okzo3wP-eVk67czDsXKfRR0kSyI6hAKhn413zgRXy))

A location can have substantial pedestrian traffic and still have poor **qualified traffic**.

**Diagnosis:** Boba traffic, mall traffic, tourist traffic, or café traffic does not automatically translate into customers willing to pay for traditional Chinese tea.

---

## 4. Dependence on one revenue stream

The durable businesses generally combine several of the following:

- Tea service
- Loose-leaf retail
- E-commerce
- Wholesale
- Teaware
- Food
- Classes
- Paid tastings
- Events
- Memberships
- Subscriptions
- Corporate gifting
- Private experiences

Seven Cups, West China Tea, Té Company, Steep LA, Tao of Tea, and Tea Drunk all currently operate multiple revenue channels. ([Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/about-us/?srsltid=AfmBOopy2thzyEdfbBo-Q597KKz6ibN7PfhDrL5EHPsK-O_-gB-pmUFj); [West China Tea](https://westchinatea.com/?srsltid=AfmBOoqU2wZf2MyYwV0pKYVd-1-ry1YtTB8uFbuzAeMteD7OoJUBMrad); [Té Company](https://tecompanytea.com/pages/tea-shop?srsltid=AfmBOopxljRdLFOfjtyhVcJe6ZELttT6bQGZCTyTTPDSTSVZJyGxZkJw); [Steep LA](https://steepla.com/pages/steep-after-dark); [The Tao Of Tea](https://taooftea.com/); [Tea Drunk](https://www.teadrunk.com/pages/private-event?srsltid=AfmBOoozxHjrU9OZOVhdqUf5jf16DyPZaukrkXPH871pEqs5iBMlK48d))

**Diagnosis:** A room supported only by individual pots of tea is the most fragile version of the business.

---

## 5. The founder is often part of the product

This may be the most important issue for the proposed remotely managed LLC structure.

At Tea Habitat, the opportunity to drink with the owner and learn from her was described as the central attraction. West China Tea’s founder personally cultivated the Austin community. Tea Drunk’s educational identity is strongly attached to its founder’s knowledge. Seven Cups was built through its founders’ sourcing credibility and cultural direction. ([Tea Habitat](https://teahabitat.com/blogs/art-of-tea/at-tea-habitat-tea-connoisseurship-is-taken-to-the-extreme?srsltid=AfmBOopoU-JHp60okzo3wP-eVk67czDsXKfRR0kSyI6hAKhn413zgRXy); [Voyage Austin](https://voyageaustin.com/interview/hidden-gems-meet-so-han-fan-of-west-china-tea/); [Tea Drunk](https://www.teadrunk.com/pages/educational-tea-club?srsltid=AfmBOooiYNf906sc4ilMG5ITOICbXXjeBfdEl2i_gDJE0LMgoMtDCDdY); [Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/2022/01/happy-chinese-new-year-from-seven-cups/?srsltid=AfmBOoo5Zl7F7FkyyoQCCXwIT8uE55Pit-xac9QSqYXNyzburVFTi9qW))

That means a manager is not merely handling scheduling and inventory. The manager may need to deliver:

- Tea knowledge
- Hospitality
- Cultural interpretation
- Storytelling
- Community leadership
- Customer trust
- Brand personality

**Diagnosis:** A remote-owned store with an ordinary retail manager is not the same model as these founder-led teahouses.

This needs to be tested before leasing anything. A local operator should successfully lead paid events without the founder physically present.

---

## 6. China-dependent inventory creates geopolitical exposure

Denong’s 2026 suspension resulted directly from its inability to replenish U.S. inventory. West China Tea publicly described the effect of tariff changes because its tea and most of its teaware come from China. ([Denong Tea](https://www.denongtea.com/pages/temporary-pause-in-u-s-shipments); [KEYE](https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/90-day-tariff-pause-brings-temporary-relief-to-austin-tea-house-preparing-to-re-open))

**Diagnosis:** Cultural authenticity and direct sourcing create differentiation, but they can also create freight, customs, tariff, food-compliance, currency, and political risk.

---

## 7. A strong community can still be economically difficult

West China Tea and Ching Ching Cha demonstrate that emotional loyalty, repeat visitors, and cultural importance do not guarantee easy economics. Communities tend to want long stays, accessible prices, and an unhurried atmosphere—the exact conditions that can restrict revenue per square foot. ([Voyage Austin](https://voyageaustin.com/interview/hidden-gems-meet-so-han-fan-of-west-china-tea/); [The Georgetown Voice](https://georgetownvoice.com/2023/02/03/for-authenticity-ching-ching-cha-stay-it-had-to-leave/))

**Diagnosis:** Community value and commercial value overlap, but they are not identical.

The business must decide who pays for maintaining the community space: members, event customers, product buyers, corporate clients, or ordinary daily visitors.

# The modern Chinese-chain comparison

CHAGEE opened its first U.S. location in Los Angeles in 2025. By June 1, 2026, it reportedly had nine U.S. locations, while its current official locator lists eleven California stores. ([Eater Los Angeles](https://la.eater.com/2025/3/26/24394652/chagee-china-tea-shop-opening-us-los-angeles-trump-trade-war); [Restaurant Business Online](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/financing/chagee-cites-early-success-its-us-locations); [Chagee](https://www.chagee.us/stores))

This is real evidence that American consumers will pay for Chinese-branded tea beverages. But it is **not direct validation of a traditional teahouse**.

CHAGEE sells familiar, portable products such as tea lattes, milk teas, matcha drinks, and blended beverages. The customer does not need a lesson, a reservation, or an hour-long session to purchase one. ([Eater Los Angeles](https://la.eater.com/2025/3/26/24394652/chagee-china-tea-shop-opening-us-los-angeles-trump-trade-war); [Chagee](https://chagee.us/))

The modern chain model removes friction:

- Clear individual drinks
- Familiar sweetness and milk
- Quick ordering
- Takeaway convenience
- Strong visual branding
- Easy repeat purchase
- No requirement to understand gongfu tea

Therefore, boba and modern Chinese tea chains are useful evidence of **openness to Asian beverages**, but weak evidence for the economics of a traditional cultural experience.

# What this changes in our original thesis

## “Do not open in Utah” is too absolute

The research weakens that categorical statement.

Seven Cups is one of the most durable traditional Chinese-tea businesses in the country, and it is in Tucson. West China Tea developed a meaningful community in Austin before Austin became an obvious national retail market. ([Seven Cups](https://sevencups.com/2022/01/happy-chinese-new-year-from-seven-cups/?srsltid=AfmBOoo5Zl7F7FkyyoQCCXwIT8uE55Pit-xac9QSqYXNyzburVFTi9qW); [Voyage Austin](https://voyageaustin.com/interview/hidden-gems-meet-so-han-fan-of-west-china-tea/))

This does not establish that Utah is a good first market. It means city rankings alone cannot answer the question.

The better test is:

> **Can she build a concentrated group of paying repeat customers in that city before opening a store?**

Utah should be rejected because a real test fails—not merely because another city has more boba shops.

---

# The model I would copy

The best path is not to copy one company exactly. It is to combine the strongest parts of several:

**Seven Cups:** build community and product demand before the lease.

**Té Company:** begin with online and wholesale, then open a very small room.

**Tea Habitat:** make advanced experiences appointment-based and explicitly paid.

**Tea Drunk:** turn knowledge into classes, memberships, content, and private events.

**Steep LA:** create accessible entry products and additional reasons to visit.

**West China Tea:** build a genuine community, but avoid copying the premature jump from 200 to 3,000 square feet.

The sequence should be:

> **Content and education → sampler and online sales → paid events → wholesale and corporate experiences → compact appointment studio → public-facing store only after the economics are proven.**

---

# The model I would avoid

I would avoid:

- A large “beautiful Chinese teahouse” as the first experiment
- Several locations opened close together
- A mall store dependent on unqualified walk-in traffic
- A business supported only by pots of tea
- A remote founder relying on a generic store manager
- A lease based on optimistic traffic forecasts
- Direct importing before identifying repeat-purchase products
- A large inventory of rare tea selected according to expert taste but not customer behavior
- Assuming that social-media attention equals purchases
- Assuming that boba demand equals traditional-tea demand

---

# New validation gates suggested by the precedents

Before considering a permanent store, I would require:

1. **At least 20 paid tea events in one metro**, with stable attendance and pricing.
2. **At least eight events led entirely by the prospective local manager**, without the founder present.
3. **At least 300 unique paying local customers**, not social followers or free attendees.
4. **A measurable 90-day repeat-purchase rate of approximately 30% or better.**
5. **Multiple functioning revenue streams:** events, take-home tea, e-commerce, gifting, memberships, or wholesale.
6. **Evidence that customers buy tea after the experience**, rather than treating the tasting as a one-time cultural novelty.
7. **A compact-room financial model that pays the manager a real market salary.**
8. **At least two viable U.S. supply arrangements**, rather than complete dependence on one direct shipping route from China.
9. **A downside model that survives a relocation delay, tariff increase, or 30% revenue shortfall.**
10. **A clear answer to whether the customer is buying tea, education, relaxation, social connection, cultural discovery, or some combination.**

These are proposed decision gates, not industry averages.

# Final judgment

The prior attempts do **not** show that Chinese tea culture has no market in America.

They show something more specific:

> **Chinese tea can support a durable niche brand, a respected educational business, an online retailer, an appointment tasting room, a community club, or a hybrid hospitality concept. It has much less evidence supporting a large, remotely managed, pure traditional teahouse whose primary revenue comes from walk-in tea service.**

The most serious hidden risk is not the LLC structure. It is that the founder’s knowledge, personality, taste, and hospitality may be the core product—and those qualities cannot automatically be delegated to a store manager.

So the decisive experiment is no longer just, “Will Americans pay for this tea?”

It is:

> **“Can a trained local operator repeatedly deliver the experience, create customer loyalty, and sell the products without the founder being physically present?”**

Until that has been demonstrated through paid manager-led events, the store is still premature.
